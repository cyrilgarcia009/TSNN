Review of "Statistical Benchmarking of Transformer Models in Low Signal-to-Noise Time-Series Forecasting"
I've read through the paper carefully. Here is a comprehensive list of issues you should address before resubmitting to NeurIPS. I've organized them by severity.
Major Issues (likely reasons for rejection)
1. Limited novelty framing. The two-way (temporal + cross-sectional) attention idea is essentially what iTransformer, Crossformer, and axial attention already do. You cite Ho et al. (2019) and Liu et al. (2024), but you never clearly articulate what is architecturally new versus what is a re-evaluation. Reframe the contribution explicitly as (a) a controlled benchmarking methodology and (b) the dynamic sparsification mechanism — and de-emphasize the architecture itself as a contribution.
2. Weak baseline comparison in the main text. Your main experiments (Section 3) compare only against Lasso, Boosting, and an MLP. Comparisons to actual SOTA time-series transformers (Informer, Autoformer, FEDformer, PatchTST, iTransformer, TFT, TiDE, NBEATSx, DLinear) are buried in Appendix C and D, are incomplete (e.g., Appendix C is missing the Linear effect column and missing the Trans model on some rows compared to the main tables), and use only a subset of effects. Reviewers will see this as cherry-picking. Move SOTA comparisons into the main text and run them across the full grid.
3. The TSCS-Shift failure is unresolved and under-discussed. Your transformer fails completely on TSCS-Shift (correlations near zero or negative across all noise levels in Table 5). You acknowledge this but punt it to "future work." For NeurIPS, you need either (a) a hypothesis tested empirically about why this happens (does the alternation order TCTC matter? does CTCT help? does interleaved/joint attention help?) or (b) a proposed fix. As written, this is a substantial limitation that undermines the architectural claim.
4. Statistical significance is missing from the main results. Tables 1–7 report point estimates with no error bars, no confidence intervals, and no significance tests. You only do bootstrapping in Section 4 for the sparsity experiments. NeurIPS reviewers will expect bootstrap confidence intervals or seed-variance reporting on every table, especially given that several differences appear small relative to plausible noise (e.g., Trans 0.045 vs Lasso 0.038 at ρ=0.02 in Table 1).
5. Single-seed results are likely. It's unclear how many seeds were used for each entry in Tables 1–7. If single-seed, this is a serious methodological problem given the low SNR regime where run-to-run variance is large. Report mean ± std over at least 5–10 seeds.
6. Hyperparameter tuning fairness is unclear. Did you tune hyperparameters for the baselines (Lasso CV alpha, boosting depth/learning rate, MLP architecture) and for the transformer with the same budget? Without a clear statement, reviewers will assume you tuned the transformer more carefully than the baselines.
7. Sparsity threshold K=0.1 is unjustified. You set K=0.1 throughout and acknowledge it could be learned or cross-validated, but never do an ablation. A sensitivity analysis over K (e.g., 0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5) is essential to show the method isn't fragile.
8. The M5 experiment (Appendix D) has a major red flag. Ridge regression has a Train Correlation of 1.000 and a Test Correlation of 0.024 — that's catastrophic overfitting and suggests the Ridge baseline is essentially unregularized or the regularization wasn't properly cross-validated. This makes the comparison unfair and undermines the credibility of the M5 result. Either fix the Ridge setup or remove this table.
Methodological Issues
9. Train/test split methodology. With T_train=2500 and T_test=1500, is the test set the chronologically later portion? Is there any validation split for early stopping? Currently unclear.
10. No early stopping or training protocol described. How many epochs? What learning rate? What optimizer? What batch size? Section 2.5 describes architecture but not training. Reproducibility is impossible as written.
11. The "global" labelling is inconsistent. You describe global Lasso (one model for all series) but the OLS in Appendix B fits one model per series. The MLP is described as global. Be explicit about which baseline uses which paradigm and why.
12. The OLS theoretical bound (TheoC) appears across all tables but it's only valid for the Linear effect. Including it as a row for non-linear effects (Tables 4, 5) is misleading — readers will think TheoC is a meaningful benchmark for those effects. Either restrict it to Table 1, or clearly footnote that it is a per-effect-irrelevant reference for non-linear cases.
13. The features X are sampled i.i.d. N(0,1) across time. This means the predictors have no autocorrelation, which is unrealistic for real time-series and makes the TS-Shift problem pathological — every lag is independent noise. A more realistic setup would use AR(1) or similar processes for X. At minimum, acknowledge this limitation prominently.
14. The bootstrap in Section 4 regenerates the entire dataset each iteration. This measures variance over data-generating processes, not over model training stochasticity. Both sources of variance matter. Clarify what your bootstrap is actually measuring.
15. Two-sample test details missing. What test? t-test, Welch, Mann-Whitney? With n=90, this matters for the small-effect claims. Specify and justify.
16. The "deterministic sparse" upper bound is only shown for TS-Shift (Table 8). Adding this for CS-Shift (where you say it's not as obvious) and other effects would strengthen the claim that max sparse approaches the oracle.
Presentation and Writing Issues
17. Abstract over-claims. "Two-way attention transformers... can outperform standard baselines" — but Lasso beats the transformer on Linear and TS-Shift effects. Soften to "can outperform... on certain dependency structures, particularly cross-sectional and non-linear."
18. The phrase "low signal-to-noise" is used loosely. Define quantitatively in the abstract (e.g., "ρ as low as 0.02").
19. Notation inconsistencies. ρ is used both for the global SNR and for per-feature coefficients ρ_{j,n}. Use different symbols.
20. Equation (2) constraint issue. You write ρ² = Σ ρ_e², but for arbitrary effects with arbitrary correlations between Ỹ_e components, this normalization doesn't automatically hold. Either prove orthogonality or state the assumption.
21. Tables 12 and 13 appear without surrounding interpretation. The reader has to figure out themselves what "intuitive shape" the temporal matrix should have. Add a panel showing the optimal/ground-truth attention matrix alongside the learned one.
22. Figures are absent. The paper has zero figures. For NeurIPS, attention matrix heatmaps (instead of numeric tables 12–13), performance curves vs ρ, and sparsity-vs-K curves would dramatically improve readability.
23. "Trans" is a poor model name. Use something more descriptive like "TC-Transformer" or "BiAttn" given the term's other connotations.
24. The Impact Statement is perfunctory. NeurIPS expects a more substantive broader-impact discussion, including potential misuse in financial forecasting (where low-SNR claims could mislead users into believing real signal exists).
25. Grammatical and typographical issues. Examples: "a full attention full attention transformer" (Section 4.3, doubled phrase), "deterministically sparse deterministic sparse transformer" (same issue), "sparisty" (typo), "CS- Shift" (errant space). A careful copy-edit pass is needed.
26. Citation formatting in references is inconsistent. Some entries lack venues; others have abbreviations.
Missing Content
27. No ablation on the attention block ordering. You use "TCTC" but never test "CTCT", "TTCC", "TC", "TCTCTC", etc. The choice of ordering is a key architectural decision.
28. No ablation on number of heads, d_model, FFN dim. Reviewers will ask whether the transformer's performance is sensitive to these.
29. No analysis of computational cost. A central critique of transformers in time-series is the cost vs. linear models. Report parameters, training time, and inference time.
30. No comparison to DLinear / NLinear (Zeng et al. 2023, which you cite). This is the most important "transformers are not effective" baseline and is conspicuously absent.
31. Look-back window T_win=10 is very short. Real time-series transformer benchmarks use 96, 192, 336, 720. Justify this choice or run with longer windows.
32. Multi-step forecasting is not addressed. The paper does only one-step-ahead. Most time-series transformer literature does multi-step. State this scope limitation explicitly.
33. The connection to "sparsity-inducing regularization in classical regression" claimed in the abstract is never developed. Either develop it (it would be interesting) or remove the claim.
34. Appendix C tables are confusingly formatted. Where is the "Linear" effect row? Why is "TCTC" the column header instead of "Trans" as in the main paper? Why doesn't this table also include Boosting and MLP for direct comparison with main results?
Smaller Issues
35. "Order 0/1/2" terminology is your own invention and could be confused with other uses (e.g., differencing orders in ARIMA). Consider renaming.
36. The claim that "10 years of daily data" is your motivating regime appears in the abstract and Section 2.3 but is never tied back to a real-world dataset experiment in the main text (only in M5 appendix, which has the issues noted above).
37. Equation (5) modulo-N for cross-sectional shifts is a strange choice — it implies the series have a meaningful circular ordering, which is unusual. Justify or use a different scheme.
38. In Section 4.2, "We do not perform this optimization here" regarding K — for a NeurIPS submission this is not acceptable; do the cross-validation.
39. The p-values in Tables 8–11 are inconsistent in interpretation. In Table 8, p=0.028 at ρ=0.015 is described as "high level of significance" — is this comparing max_sparse vs full_attention, or vs deterministic_sparse? Clarify what is being tested in each cell.
40. No discussion of why two-way attention might be theoretically better than alternatives like full joint attention over flattened (T_win × N) tokens. A brief theoretical motivation would help.

Suggested Priorities for Resubmission
If I had to rank the most impactful changes: (1) add SOTA baselines to the main text with proper hyperparameter tuning, (2) add multi-seed results with confidence intervals everywhere, (3) fix the M5 Ridge issue or remove that section, (4) investigate or propose a fix for the TSCS-Shift failure, (5) add an ablation on K and on attention block ordering, and (6) convert Tables 12–13 into proper figures with ground-truth comparison.
The core idea (controlled synthetic benchmarking + dynamic sparsity) is interesting and worth publishing, but the execution has gaps that NeurIPS reviewers will likely flag aggressively. Want me to dig deeper on any specific issue or help draft revised sections?
